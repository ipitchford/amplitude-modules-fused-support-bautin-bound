Abstract

We formulate two Atlas-derived amplitude-classification theorem candidates and one externally motivated ordinary coefficient-Bautin theorem candidate. We first reconstruct the load-bearing polynomial-amplitude identity from the Cyclicity Support-Fusion Atlas [14] self-containedly: a formal moment transform identifies the twisted quotient with distinct Kummer channels, a corrected falling-factorial Vandermonde argument computes cyclic rank, and Fourier inversion identifies the same channels with primitive root values. We then derive a general monodromy-block decomposition: central monodromy projectors descend to free polynomial modules, and every nonzero block component activates its entire Fourier-character block. For every nondegenerate Dickson phase Dd(x,a)D_d(x,a), meaning a≠0a\ne0, we then give a free 𝑪[Dd]\mathbf C[D_d]-module decomposition of arbitrary polynomial primitives and prove the exact rank formula q=2N+εq=2N+\varepsilon. This yields a parity classification: odd degree realizes precisely the even ranks, while even degree realizes every rank. Second, for the exceptional Ritt collision P=(x4+x)3P=(x^4+x)^3, we exhibit three explicit fused amplitude modules and prove that every nonzero element of each activates exactly three channels. Together with the two composition channels, these modules partition all eleven non-trace residues and realize every rank. Third, for every degree-dd polynomial phase, every zero-cycle, and polynomial perturbations of arbitrary fixed degree, we prove that the ordinary coefficient Bautin ideal of the zero-cycle displacement is generated by Melnikov orders one through d−1d-1. The proof combines an all-order Lagrange–Bürmann identity, injectivity of differentiation on zero-cycle polynomial integrals, and a Cayley–Hamilton recurrence. Writing bcoeff(m)b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m) for this explicit ordinary coefficient index, we obtain bcoeff(m)≤m−1b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m)\le m-1. If the terse index b(m)b(m) in [17] is intended in this coefficient sense, then the same bound answers its Problem 8.4. The d−1d-1 cutoff is sharp on the complexified cycle module. For integral cycles we prove only the lower witness d/pd/p, where pp is the least prime divisor of dd. As supporting infrastructure, we also identify Atlas support with the minimal order of an operator annihilating the complete rapid-decay period system and retain corrected cycle-specific block bounds. Exact finite checks support the algebraic arguments but are not substitutes for proof.

1. Introduction

Let P∈𝑪[x]P\in\mathbf C[x] be a polynomial phase, let A∈𝑪[x]A\in\mathbf C[x] be an amplitude, and consider rapid-decay exponential periods

IΓ,A(s)=∫ΓA(x)esP(x)dx. I_{\Gamma,A}(s)=\int_\Gamma A(x)e^{sP(x)}\,dx.

The number and structure of independent channels activated by AA govern both the geometry of the primitive GG, where G′=AG'=A, and the differential equations satisfied by the complete period system. The Cyclicity Support-Fusion Atlas [14] proposes a particularly sharp bridge: cyclic rank equals the number of non-trace inverse-series residues and also the reduced span of the primitive values on a generic fibre. Section 2 reconstructs that bridge as a theorem within this manuscript rather than importing it as a premise.

The Atlas leaves an open direction around classification by induced rank, support fusion and period order. Problems A and B below are two explicit amplitude-classification subproblems of that Atlas direction. Problem C is instead an ordinary coefficient-Bautin theorem candidate motivated by the externally posed Problem 8.4 of [17]. The period-order construction is supporting infrastructure, not a fourth headline claim.

Problem A. Classify the cyclic rank of every polynomial amplitude for the nondegenerate Dickson phase Dd(x,a)D_d(x,a), with a≠0a\ne0, uniformly in degree and parameter.

Problem B. Classify every polynomial amplitude for the degree-twelve exceptional Ritt collision P=(x4+x)3P=(x^4+x)^3, including hidden cancellation inside the fused blocks.

Problem C. For phases and perturbations of degree at most mm, and every zero-cycle of the phase, bound the ordinary coefficient Bautin index of the displacement uniformly in the phase, perturbation and cycle.

Support-to-order bridge. Given arbitrary P,A∈𝑸[x]P,A\in\mathbf Q[x], compute a primitive scalar operator for every rapid-decay period, prove its complete-system minimal order, predict that order from inverse support, and bound the order for a specified ray-cycle.

Our answers have a common pattern. We first identify the active inverse residues with the cyclic summands of the twisted de Rham quotient. We then use monodromy and explicit polynomial modules to classify those residues in the two structured families, use exact quotient reduction to construct the period operator, and use a second quotient algebra to control all Melnikov orders. The reconstructed support proof follows the Atlas proof architecture and was produced by the same research system, so it is self-contained but not an independent validation of the Atlas.

The organizing principle is finite-fibre algebra. On the exponential-period side, the rank-(d−1)(d-1) twisted de Rham quotient turns the infinite sequence [Adx],[PAdx],…[A\,dx],[PA\,dx],\ldots into a finite cyclic-module problem. On the zero-cycle side, the rank-dd algebra R[t,x]/(f−t)R[t,x]/(f-t) turns the infinite power-moment sequence into a finite characteristic recurrence. Monodromy then supplies the missing information: Fourier support in the first quotient and injectivity of differentiation in the second. The two constructions are different instances of the same finite-cover mechanism rather than unrelated applications.

1.1. Main results

For an arbitrary degree-dd phase, the inertia dd-cycle has simple Fourier spectrum. The monodromy permutation module is therefore multiplicity-free and splits into character blocks. Using the reconstructed root-span/support equality, central projectors descend to free 𝑪[P]\mathbf C[P]-modules, and the attainable supports are exactly the unions of these blocks. This supplies the organizing principle for the two explicit classifications below.

For a≠0a\ne0, every primitive has a unique Dickson expansion in paired modules. Each nonzero pair activates both conjugate residue channels, because losing either would force a nonsplit quadratic element into the rational base field. This proves

q(Dd,G′dx)=2N(G)+ε(G). q(D_d,G'\,dx)=2N(G)+\varepsilon(G).

For Q=x4+xQ=x^4+x and P=Q3P=Q^3, we construct three rank-three modules

M0=⟨x,x7+74x4,x10−52x4⟩,M1=⟨x2,x5,x11+114x8⟩,M2=⟨x3,x6,x9⟩. \begin{aligned} M_0&=\langle x,\ x^7+\tfrac74x^4,\ x^{10}-\tfrac52x^4\rangle,\\ M_1&=\langle x^2,\ x^5,\ x^{11}+\tfrac{11}{4}x^8\rangle,\\ M_2&=\langle x^3,\ x^6,\ x^9\rangle. \end{aligned}

The quartic has monodromy S4S_4, whose reduced permutation representation is irreducible. This forces all three non-trace quartic channels. A fibre-trace lemma then removes the additional quartic trace channel after composition, while a modulo-three grading fuses the surviving channels into three explicit blocks modulo twelve.

For the third result, let

be the displacement of a zero-cycle under a generic polynomial perturbation g𝒂g_{\boldsymbol a} of arbitrary fixed degree. Parameter content is the ideal generated by the polynomial coefficients of an algebraic function when expanded in any complex basis of its finite coefficient span. If ℬk\mathcal B_k is generated by the contents of M1,…,MkM_1,\ldots,M_k, then

ℬ∞=ℬd−1. \mathcal B_\infty=\mathcal B_{d-1}.

Differentiation is injective on the vector space of zero-cycle polynomial integrals: a differentiated integral in the kernel would be a single-valued polynomial in tt, and averaging its infinity-monodromy orbit annihilates the zero-cycle. Hence differentiation preserves parameter content exactly. Cayley–Hamilton for multiplication by gg in the rank-dd algebra R[t,x]/(f−t)R[t,x]/(f-t) then shows that no power moment, and therefore no Melnikov coefficient, introduces a generator beyond order d−1d-1. As a supporting bridge, the first dependence among

in the twisted quotient produces an operator annihilating every rapid-decay period. Using the reconstructed theorem and Hien’s perfect pairing between algebraic de Rham cohomology and rapid-decay homology, its minimal complete-system order is

|Σ(P;G)|=dim⁡span⁡red{G(xi)}. |\Sigma(P;G)| =\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathrm{red}}\{G(x_i)\}.

Full contact or differential-module irreducibility makes the appropriate bound sharp. This is a qualified individual-cycle statement, not the blanket channel-count formula rejected below and not the third headline claim.

1.2. Prior-art boundary

Puiseux expansions of Q(Pi−1)Q(P_i^{-1}), Fourier support separation, monodromy subspaces and zero-dimensional Abelian integrals are established in Pakovich–Muzychuk [1], Alvarez Sanchez–Bravo Trinidad–Mardesic [2], and Gavrilov–Pakovich [3]. Pakovich’s fixed-segment Chebyshev moment theorem [13] gives a Chebyshev-derivative basis for the amplitudes whose moments all vanish; it is a close structural antecedent but does not state the cyclic rank of every amplitude for the complete rapid-decay period system. Individual Chebyshev/Lucas amplitudes in exponential integrals and their Bessel or Airy–Lucas equations occur in [4,5].

Pakovich–Muzychuk [1] also classify invariant subspaces of a polynomial monodromy group containing a full cycle. The representation-theoretic block decomposition in Section 2.1 is therefore not claimed as new. The residual steps used here are the descent of central projectors to polynomial 𝑪[P]\mathbf C[P]-lattices, realization of every nonzero lattice component as the full inverse-support character block, and the resulting all-amplitude rank formulas.

The exact phase (x4+x)3(x^4+x)^3 is already identified as a Ritt collision with monodromy S4×C3S_4\times C_3 by Behajaina–Konig–Neftin [6, Theorems 2.2(1) and 2.3(1)]. Our residual candidate contribution in Problem B is therefore the amplitude-module/support classification and all-amplitude rank formula, not the collision or its monodromy group.

Minimal hyperexponential telescopers from first reduced dependence are prior art [7], as are order-aware and reduction-based creative-telescoping methods [8,9]. Rapid-decay period representations for exponential Gauss–Manin systems are also established [10], and Hien proves the required perfect period pairing [12, Theorem 5.2]. The support-to-order bridge is presented only as the Atlas-specific identity and an exact benchmark.

Bravo–Mardešić–Novikov–Pontigo-Herrera [17] ask for a degree-uniform zero-cycle Bautin bound. Under the ordinary Taylor-coefficient reading of their ideal chain, Proposition 6.1 identifies that coefficient ideal with the parameter-content ideal used here and Problem C gives bcoeff(m)≤m−1b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m)\le m-1. If [17] intends its terse b(m)b(m) in this sense, then b(m)=bcoeff(m)b(m)=b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m). Batenkov–Yomdin [18] define ordinary Bautin ideals by Taylor coefficients and prove a general Bautin-ideal theorem for polynomial recurrences, while Briskin–Yomdin [19] treat algebraic families of analytic functions. These are close antecedents. The residual step here is to prove that the order-dependent differentiation taking power moments to Melnikov coefficients preserves parameter content on a zero-cycle. The source [17] does not specify a different localization, radicalization, or analytic-parameter ideal; none of those alternatives is claimed here. The bounded search found no exact statement of the d−1d-1 bound, but does not establish priority.

The broader block theorem, the cycle-by-cycle order question and the binomial Laurent specialization were also tested during development. The manuscript gives self-contained arguments for its principal retained candidate conclusions. The unrestricted exact cycle formula fails by the counterexample in Section 8.1; only the proved standard-ray block bound is retained. The former general Dickson individual-cycle equality was removed because its scalar differential-module derivation was incomplete. The binomial result appears in Appendix A as a direct Pakovich-based corollary, not as a fourth novelty claim.

8. Discussion

The Dickson result turns pairwise Laurent symmetry into a uniform all-amplitude classification. Its parity effect is easy to miss if one checks only even degrees: odd phases have no midpoint and therefore cannot have odd rank.

The exceptional Ritt result shows a different fusion mechanism. The three correction-polynomial modules are not composition summands. Their rigidity comes from the irreducible standard representation of the quartic monodromy, while the outer cube imposes a modulo-three grading. The result is a complete support partition rather than a list of isolated examples.

The coefficient-Bautin theorem is the broadest of the three claims. Its content is not Noetherianity alone: Cayley–Hamilton gives a finite recurrence, and infinity monodromy shows that the order-dependent differentiation in the Melnikov formula preserves the ordinary parameter ideal exactly. This produces an explicit linear bound for the full degree box, not only a description of its centre set.

The weighted order theorem is conceptually useful even though its reduction algorithm is not new. Creative telescoping discovers the first dependence; the reconstructed support quantity predicts its order from inverse support or root values. Chen et al. [16] explain non-minimality of individual-contour telescopers through residues in the rational setting. Their result is a close discrete analogue of the residue obstruction below, not a prior statement of our complete-system support identity. That prediction is the narrow bridge proposed here.

8.1. Why raw channel count is not individual-period order

A stronger formula considered during development would make the minimal order of an individual period equal the number of residue channels left active by its cycle DFT. That statement is false.

For P=D3(x,1)=x3−3xP=D_3(x,1)=x^3-3x, A=1A=1, a complex zero-cycle can isolate one of the two residues of G=xG=x. The isolated function nevertheless satisfies the modified Bessel equation

f″+1sf′−(4+19s2)f=0, f''+\frac1sf'-\left(4+\frac{1}{9s^2}\right)f=0,

which has no first-order factor over 𝑪(s)\mathbf C(s). A rational logarithmic derivative would have residue ±1/3\pm1/3 at zero, residue 11 at every other finite pole, and total finite residue −1/2-1/2 from its expansion at infinity—an impossibility. Differentiation preserves the residue label but does not preserve the one-dimensional span of a channel function.

This adverse example is why no unrestricted equality with |R||R| is claimed. It is consistent with the repaired bound (5.4): one touched channel lies inside a two-dimensional Dickson block, so the upper bound is two, and the explicit irreducible Bessel equation makes the actual order two.

wN/h=mcn, w^{N/h}=\frac{m}{cn},

after cancellation of the common factor. They are simple, and their critical values are distinct because exponentiation by −m/h-m/h is injective on the (N/h)(N/h)-th-root orbit. Thus the connected monodromy group is transitive and generated by transpositions, hence is the natural symmetric group.
