## Exact result

Let \(f\) be monic of degree \(d\ge2\), let \(C\) be any zero-cycle of
\(f\), and let

\[
g_{\boldsymbol a}(x)=\sum_{r=0}^n a_rx^r
\]

have arbitrary fixed degree \(n\).  If
\(\mathcal B_k\subset\mathbf C[\boldsymbol a]\) is the ordinary ideal
generated by the parameter content of the first \(k\) Melnikov coefficients,
then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal B_\infty=\mathcal B_{d-1}.}
\]

Consequently, for the degree box \(\deg f,\deg g\le m\),

\[
\boxed{b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m)\le m-1.}
\]

The written candidate proof uses the all-order identity

\[
M_\mu=\frac{(-1)^\mu}{\mu!}D_t^{\mu-1}\int_Cg^\mu.
\]

Differentiation is injective on the space
\(\{\int_Ch:h\in\mathbf C[x]\}\): a kernel element would be a single-valued
polynomial in \(t\), while averaging its orbit under the \(d\)-cycle at
infinity annihilates the zero-cycle.  Therefore differentiation preserves
parameter content exactly.  Cayley--Hamilton for multiplication by \(g\) in
\(R[t,x]/(f(x)-t)\) then generates every power-moment content ideal from the
first \(d-1\), with no restriction on \(\deg g\).

The bound is sharp for complex-weighted cycles: for \(f=x^d\), \(g=ax\) and
\(n_j=\omega^j\), the first nonzero order is \(d-1\).  For integer-weighted
cycles, the retained construction gives first order \(d/p\), where \(p\) is
the least prime divisor of \(d\).  No integer-cycle sharpness claim is made.

## Scope and target match

Bravo--Mardešić--Novikov--Pontigo-Herrera ask for a bound when
\(\deg f,\deg g\le m\), uniformly over cycles.  The theorem covers this full
degree box for the explicitly defined ordinary Taylor-coefficient index
\(b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m)\).  The content/Taylor lemma proves that this
coefficient ideal equals the
parameter-content ideal used in the recurrence proof, with equality preserved
under localization of the parameter ring.  Equality of radicals and centre
loci follows for this convention.  Ideals formed in a larger analytic-function
ring, radicalization before contraction, and singular-base-point conventions
remain outside the claim.  If the terse \(b(m)\) in the source denotes this
ordinary coefficient ideal, then
\(b(m)=b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m)\) and the candidate answers its Problem 8.4.

Batenkov--Yomdin's polynomial-recurrence theorem and Briskin--Yomdin's
algebraic-family framework are close prior art.  The candidate residual step
is the monodromy-injectivity argument transferring the recurrence ideal
through the order-dependent derivatives.  Search absence does not establish
priority.

## Bound proof and evidence

- `THEOREM_D_BAUTIN_BOUND.md`: complete written candidate proof and claim
  boundary.
- `check_general_bautin_bound.py`: generic rectangular quotient checks.
- `general_bautin_bound_receipt.json`: deterministic exact receipt.
- `screen_power_bautin_uniform.py`: rectangular power-phase ideal and
  cycle-domain falsifiers.
- `power_bautin_uniform_receipt.json`: deterministic exact receipt.
- `check_bautin_content_bridge.py`: non-power direct/content/Taylor bridge check.
- `bautin_content_bridge_receipt.json`: deterministic exact bridge receipt.
- a demoted sextic worked case is retained privately and is not part of the
  public package.

The finite checks verify Cayley--Hamilton in cases with
\(\deg g>\deg f\), the monodromy-average identity, representative higher-order
ideal reductions, the direct/content/Taylor bridge on \(f=x^2+x\) with a
generic quartic perturbation, and the separation between integer and complex witnesses.
They do not prove the universal theorem.
