# Supplement: binomial Laurent moment channels

**Date:** 27 August 2026  
**Status:** proved as a specialization of Pakovich's Laurent-moment theorem
plus deck grading  
**Novelty disposition:** useful contextual corollary; not counted as a new
main result

## 1. Statement

Let

\[
L(z)=z^{-m}+cz^n,\qquad m,n\ge1,\quad c\ne0,
\]

put \(s=\gcd(m,n)\) and \(N=m+n\), and define

\[
\mathcal A(L)=
\left\{
A(z)\,dz:
A\in\mathbf C[z,z^{-1}],\
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}L(z)^kA(z)\,dz=0
\text{ for every }k\ge1
\right\}.
\]

Grade a monomial one-form \(z^\ell dz\) by
\(\ell\pmod s\).  Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(L)=
\bigoplus_{\ell\not\equiv-1\pmod s}
\mathbf C[z,z^{-1}]_\ell\,dz
\ \oplus\ d\mathbf C[L],
}                                                        \tag{1}
\]

where the first sum denotes all non-residual deck grades.  As a
\(\mathbf C[L]\)-module,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf C[L]}\mathcal A(L)
=\frac{(s-1)N}{s}+1.
}                                                        \tag{2}
\]

In particular, when \(\gcd(m,n)=1\),

\[
\mathcal A(L)=d\mathbf C[L].
\]

## 2. Automatic deck-graded solutions

Let \(\omega\) be an \(s\)-th root of unity.  The rotation
\(z\mapsto\omega z\) fixes \(L\).  For a grade-\(\ell\) one-form,

\[
z^\ell dz\longmapsto\omega^{\ell+1}z^\ell dz.
\]

The residue of \(L^kz^\ell dz\) is rotation-invariant, so it equals
\(\omega^{\ell+1}\) times itself for every \(\omega\).  It therefore
vanishes unless \(\ell\equiv-1\pmod s\).  This proves the first summand in
(1).

## 3. Reduction of the residual grade

Write \(m=sm_0\), \(n=sn_0\), with
\(\gcd(m_0,n_0)=1\), and put \(w=z^s\).  Then

\[
L(z)=\widetilde L(w)=w^{-m_0}+cw^{n_0}.
\]

Every residual-grade form is a linear combination of
\(z^{sq-1}dz\), and

\[
z^{sq-1}dz=\frac1s w^{q-1}dw.
\]

Thus the residual-grade moment problem is exactly the primitive binomial
moment problem for \(\widetilde L\).

## 4. Primitive binomial monodromy

Let \(R=m_0+n_0=N/s\).  The finite critical points of
\(\widetilde L\) satisfy

\[
w^R=\frac{m_0}{cn_0}.
\]

They are simple.  At such a point,

\[
\widetilde L(w)=
\left(1+\frac{m_0}{n_0}\right)w^{-m_0}.
\]

The map \(w\mapsto w^{-m_0}\) is injective on the \(R\)-th-root orbit
because

\[
\gcd(m_0,R)=\gcd(m_0,n_0)=1.
\]

Hence the \(R\) finite critical values are distinct and their local branch
cycles are transpositions.  The rational cover is connected, so its
monodromy is transitive and generated by these finite branch cycles.  A
transitive permutation group generated by transpositions is the full
symmetric group \(S_R\), which is doubly transitive.

## 5. Application of Pakovich's theorem

Pakovich's Laurent-moment theorem [Theorem 6.5] first shows that a one-form satisfying all
moments is exact, \(A\,dz=dM\), and then states that for a proper Laurent
phase with doubly transitive monodromy the primitive must have the form

\[
M=H(\widetilde L)
\]

with \(H\) polynomial.  Therefore the primitive residual-grade solutions are
exactly \(d\mathbf C[\widetilde L]\).  Pullback by \(w=z^s\) gives
\(d\mathbf C[L]\), proving (1).

## 6. Module rank

The Laurent ring is a free \(\mathbf C[L]\)-module of rank \(N\).  One
consecutive basis is represented by the exponents

\[
-m,-m+1,\ldots,n-1;
\]

the relation \(Lz^k=z^{k-m}+cz^{k+n}\) reduces every other exponent to this
window.  Each residue class modulo \(s\) occurs \(N/s\) times in the window.

The \(s-1\) non-residual grades therefore contribute
\((s-1)N/s\) free generators.  The residual solution
\(d\mathbf C[L]=\mathbf C[L]\,dL\) contributes one, proving (2).

## 7. Prior-art and assurance boundary

The deck-grading mechanism and the doubly-transitive Laurent-moment
classification are both explicit in F. Pakovich, *On rational functions
orthogonal to all powers of a given rational function on a curve*,
arXiv:0910.2105 and Moscow Mathematical Journal 13 (2013), especially
Theorem 6.5.

The binomial observation supplies an elementary all-degree specialization:
after removing the deck factor, its monodromy is the natural symmetric group.
The present audit therefore treats (1)--(2) as a clean corollary and useful
comparison, not as an independently novel solution.  The finite-test bound
proposed in the user attachment is not included because its degree convention
and orbit-bound specialization have not been independently checked.
