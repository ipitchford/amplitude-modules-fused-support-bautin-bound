# Five-perspective simulated review synthesis

**Manuscript:** *Amplitude modules, fused support, and a coefficient-Bautin
bound for polynomial phases*  
**Panel completed:** 27 August 2026  
**Editorial disposition:** **MAJOR REVISION — HOLD FROM RELEASE**

This synthesis combines five procedurally separate, same-system simulated
readings: editor-in-chief, methodology, domain, symbolic-computation and
reproducibility, and devil's advocate.  Reviewers did not read one another's
reports before returning their own.  The exercise is not independent human
review, independent validation, peer review, formal verification, or a
historical-priority determination.

## Executive decision

The panel found a worthwhile mathematical core and did not find a fatal defect
in either all-amplitude classification or in the internally defined
coefficient-content stabilization theorem.  It nevertheless unanimously
recommends major revision.  Release is blocked by one claim-identification
problem: the manuscript has not yet established that its parameter-content
ideal is the Bautin ideal intended by the cited 2025 open problem, even though
some headline sentences say that the source problem is solved.  The live
package is also not currently integrity-clean because the panel-confirmation
edit postdates the stored manifest.

The correct ceiling at the time of the original panel was therefore:

> Two internally supported all-amplitude classifications, one internally
> supported stabilization theorem for an explicit coefficient-content ideal,
> and a qualified support-to-order bridge; bounded novelty evidence; simulated
> multi-perspective review; no independent specialist validation or claim of
> three historically posed open problems solved.

## Agreement across the panel

### Mathematical findings that survived

The methodology, domain, and devil's-advocate reviews independently agreed that:

- the corrected falling-factorial Vandermonde argument supports the
  support/rank theorem;
- the Dickson quadratic obstruction prevents one-sided channel cancellation
  for \(a\ne0\);
- the exceptional Ritt fibre-trace lemma closes the former hidden-cancellation
  gap and the trace/CRT blocks are correct;
- infinity monodromy makes differentiation injective on zero-cycle polynomial
  integrals;
- parameter content is preserved under that injective differentiation for the
  manuscript's stated definition;
- Cayley--Hamilton gives the \(d-1\) stabilization for arbitrary fixed
  perturbation degree because \(J_0=0\); and
- the complex-cycle sharpness and integer-cycle lower witness are correctly
  distinguished.

No reviewer found a counterexample to Theorem A, Theorem B, or the internal
statement \(\mathcal B_\infty=\mathcal B_{d-1}\).

### Shared release blocker

The editor, methodology specialist, domain specialist, and devil's advocate all
identified the same load-bearing issue.  The cited source writes an ideal chain
generated by function-valued Melnikov coefficients, while the manuscript
formalizes this as the ideal generated by their coordinates in a finite complex
coefficient span.  The internal theorem is precise, but the source-to-manuscript
equivalence is not yet proved.  The source's \(b(m)\le m-1\) consequence must
remain conditional until one of the following is done:

1. prove equivalence with the source's ideal, preferably through a proposition
   identifying parameter content with the ideal generated by all local Taylor
   coefficients at a regular base point; or
2. narrow every headline to the explicit coefficient-content variant and stop
   saying that the cited problem itself is solved.

### Shared novelty boundary

The editor and domain reports agree that the manuscript does not yet support
the phrase “three historically posed open problems solved.”  Theorem C responds
to an explicit published question, subject to the convention bridge.  Theorems
A and B are complete family classifications inside the Atlas's broader open
direction; the present corpus does not show that those two family-specific
questions were independently posed in prior literature.  The general block
theorem also needs a theorem-level comparison with Pakovich--Muzychuk so that
known invariant-subspace theory is separated from polynomial-lattice descent
and support realization.

## Findings raised by particular perspectives

These findings were not contradicted by another reviewer and must be resolved
or expressly demoted.

### Proof and exposition

- Define the zero-cycle displacement explicitly before deriving the all-order
  Lagrange--Bürmann formula; otherwise its sign and factorial are not
  self-contained.
- State the completion used by the formal-moment transform and the
  formal-to-analytic differential-module lemma underlying the lower bound in
  the individual-cycle order statement.
- Derive the Dickson paired-block gauge equivalence to the displayed
  modified-Bessel equation, or remove the exact individual-ray formula that
  depends on it.
- Expand central-projector descent using integrality, integral closure, and
  projective-freeness over \(\mathbf C[P]\).
- Define the scalar field in the reduced root-span and correct the description
  of \(\mathcal V_C\): it need not be finite-dimensional, although each
  parameterized expression has finite coefficient span.
- Add the monic-normalization argument before applying Theorem C to the full
  degree box.
- Standardize theorem labels and clarify that \(M_2\) traces remain supported
  in powers of \(y^3\), not literally constant after arbitrary
  \(\mathbf C[P]\)-multiplication.

### Reproducibility and packaging

- The scientific receipts and PDF regenerated byte-identically in the current
  environment, but the stored package receipt is stale after
  `REFEREE_CONFIGURATION.md` changed.
- The checker must test invariant review wording rather than the obsolete phrase
  “awaiting user confirmation.”
- Replay must cryptographically bind the executable Atlas input rather than
  trust an arbitrary extracted directory; toolchain versions also need a
  reproducible environment record.
- Public release must be assembled into an empty staging directory from an
  exact allowlist, and the checker must reject undeclared files.
- Add a non-power, \(n>d\) exact test comparing direct Melnikov coefficients,
  differentiated power moments, and parameter-content ideals.
- Expand the Dickson battery beyond \(a=1\), including non-unit rational
  parameters and an explicit \(a=0\) exclusion control.
- Distinguish command executions from artifact-comparison records, and either
  replay or remove the two currently uncontracted selection screens.

### Editorial architecture and venue

The three results can remain one paper only if the finite-fibre-algebra
principle becomes an explicit organizing thesis.  Otherwise the
coefficient-Bautin theorem should become a focused paper and the two
support-fusion classifications should remain together.  Under a *Journal of
Symbolic Computation* target, the package needs a characterized algorithmic
input/output contract and comparative value beyond theorem verification;
without that, an algebraic-analysis or differential-equations venue is likely
the better fit.  Appendix A is non-novel and non-load-bearing and should
probably return to a package note.

## Disagreements and their resolution

There was no disagreement on the final recommendation: all five perspectives
selected major revision.  The methodology reviewer labelled the convention
bridge critical; the domain reviewer labelled it major because the internal
theorem remains valid; the devil's advocate again labelled the external-claim
implication critical.  The synthesis resolves that terminology by treating it
as **critical for release under the advertised open-problem claim, but not a
refutation of the internally defined theorem**.

The reproducibility reviewer labelled the stale manifest critical.  That is
critical for the present package snapshot but is a mechanical consequence of
the review-stage edit, not evidence against a theorem.  It must be repaired
only after manuscript and review artifacts stop changing.

## Ordered revision gate

1. **Claim identification:** prove the local-Taylor/content equivalence and
   determine the exact consequence legitimately attributable to the 2025
   problem; otherwise narrow the claim.
2. **Mathematical completeness:** add the displacement identity, projector
   descent, formal cycle-order lemma, and Dickson gauge derivation or demotion.
3. **Novelty discipline:** refresh the arbitrary-degree target, compare the
   block theorem directly with prior invariant-subspace theory, and describe A
   and B as Atlas subproblem classifications unless prior posed problems are
   found.
4. **Architecture:** choose an integrated finite-fibre-algebra paper or split
   the Bautin result; remove non-load-bearing material from the submission.
5. **Computational strengthening:** add the missing cross-interface tests and
   parameter coverage.
6. **Portable package:** bind the Atlas and toolchain, stage an exact public
   allowlist, rebuild PDF/manifest/receipt, and run the full ordinary and
   optimized replay from that staged package.
7. **Targeted re-review:** re-audit the convention proposition, the exact
   cycle-order statement, the novelty delta, and the detached replay.  No
   release status is available before these pass.

## Panel verdict

**MAJOR REVISION — HOLD FROM RELEASE.**

The current results are worthwhile enough to continue.  The screening outcome
is not “discard after four hours”: it is “retain the mathematical core, stop the
publication claim at the exact point where definition alignment and release
assurance remain unproved.”
