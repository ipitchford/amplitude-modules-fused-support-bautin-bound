# A uniform coefficient-Bautin bound for polynomial zero-cycle deformations

**Status:** internally proved theorem candidate; a simulated five-perspective
panel recommended major revision, and no independent specialist review has
occurred.

## 1. Setup and coefficient convention

Let \(f\in\mathbf C[x]\) be monic of degree \(d\ge2\), with local inverse
branches \(x_1(t),\ldots,x_d(t)\) over a simply connected regular domain.  Let

\[
C(t)=\sum_{j=1}^d n_jx_j(t),\qquad \sum_{j=1}^d n_j=0,
\]

be a zero-cycle.  Fix an arbitrary perturbation degree \(n\ge0\), and write

\[
g_{\boldsymbol a}(x)=\sum_{r=0}^n a_rx^r,\qquad
R=\mathbf C[a_0,\ldots,a_n].
\]

When \(n>d\), the perturbed equation has additional roots tending to infinity.
As in the source problem, those roots carry zero weights; the continued
original \(d\) roots define the deformed cycle
\(C_\epsilon(t)=\sum_jn_jz_j(t,\epsilon)\) and the displacement

\[
\Delta_C(t,\epsilon;\boldsymbol a)
=\int_{C_\epsilon(t)}f
=-\epsilon\sum_jn_jg_{\boldsymbol a}(z_j(t,\epsilon))
=\sum_{\mu\ge1}\epsilon^\mu M_\mu(t;\boldsymbol a).
\]

For \(F\in R\otimes_{\mathbf C}\mathcal K\), where \(\mathcal K\) is the
algebra of algebraic-function germs on the domain, choose any complex basis
\(v_1,\ldots,v_s\) of the finite coefficient span of \(F\), write

\[
F=\sum_{\ell=1}^s b_\ell(\boldsymbol a)v_\ell,
\]

and define its parameter content by

\[
\operatorname{cont}_R(F)=(b_1,\ldots,b_s)\subset R.
\]

An invertible complex change of basis does not change this ideal.  Define

\[
\mathcal B_k=\sum_{\mu=1}^k\operatorname{cont}_R(M_\mu),
\qquad
\mathcal B_\infty=\sum_{\mu\ge1}\operatorname{cont}_R(M_\mu).
\]

This is the ordinary coefficient ideal of the Melnikov sequence.  It is not
silently replaced by its radical, its zero set, or a local analytic ideal.

**Lemma 1.1 (content/Taylor equivalence).**  At any regular base point
\(t_0\), if

\[
F(\boldsymbol a,t)=\sum_{k\ge0}\tau_k(\boldsymbol a)(t-t_0)^k,
\]

then

\[
\operatorname{cont}_R(F)=(\tau_0,\tau_1,\ldots).
\]

**Proof.**  Write
\(F=\sum_{\ell=1}^sb_\ell(\boldsymbol a)v_\ell(t)\) in a complex basis of
its finite coefficient span.  Every Taylor coefficient \(\tau_k\) is a
complex linear combination of the \(b_\ell\), giving one inclusion.
Conversely, the infinite Taylor-coefficient vectors of the linearly
independent analytic germs \(v_\ell\) are linearly independent.  A finite
\(s\)-by-\(s\) jet submatrix is therefore invertible and expresses every
\(b_\ell\) as a complex linear combination of finitely many \(\tau_k\).
Analytic continuation and a change of regular base point act invertibly on
the finite germ span, so the ideal is independent of those choices.
\(\square\)

Thus parameter content is exactly the ordinary local Taylor-coefficient
Bautin ideal defined in the sense of Batenkov--Yomdin.

For the comparison with the source problem, let
\(\mathcal I_k^{\mathrm{coeff}}(t_0)\subset R\) be generated by all Taylor
coefficients of all local determinations of \(M_1,\ldots,M_k\) at a regular
base point.  Lemma 1.1 gives equality of ideals
\(\mathcal I_k^{\mathrm{coeff}}(t_0)=\mathcal B_k\), independently of the
regular base point and branches.  Every parameter localization preserves this
equality.  It therefore also gives equality of radicals and centre loci under
this convention, but it does not identify an ideal formed in a larger
analytic-function ring, a radical taken before contraction to \(R\), or a
singular-base-point convention.

## 2. Main theorem

**Theorem C (uniform coefficient-Bautin bound).**  For every monic
degree-\(d\) polynomial \(f\), every zero-cycle \(C\) of \(f\), and every
polynomial perturbation family \(g_{\boldsymbol a}\) of arbitrary fixed
degree,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal B_\infty=\mathcal B_{d-1}.}
\]

Consequently, if both \(f\) and \(g\) have degree at most \(m\), the ordinary
coefficient Bautin index satisfies

\[
b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m)\le m-1.
\]

Here \(b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m)\) denotes the explicit ordinary
Taylor-coefficient index above.  If the terse ideal chain in
Bravo--Mardešić--Novikov--Pontigo-Herrera is intended as that contracted
coefficient ideal, Lemma 1.1 gives
\(b(m)=b_{\mathrm{coeff}}(m)\) and the theorem answers their degree-box
problem.  The source does not settle that ambient-ring interpretation;
alternative analytic-function-ring, pre-contraction radical, and
singular-base-point conventions remain outside this statement.

## 3. All-order Melnikov identity

Put

\[
J_\mu(t;\boldsymbol a)
=\int_Cg_{\boldsymbol a}^{\mu}
=\sum_{j=1}^d n_jg_{\boldsymbol a}(x_j(t))^\mu.
\]

Branchwise Lagrange--Bürmann inversion gives, for every \(\mu\ge1\),

\[
M_\mu(t;\boldsymbol a)
=\frac{(-1)^\mu}{\mu!}
\left(\frac d{dt}\right)^{\mu-1}J_\mu(t;\boldsymbol a).       \tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed, if \(G_j(u)=g_{\boldsymbol a}(x_j(u))\) and
\(u_j+\epsilon G_j(u_j)=t\), Lagrange--Bürmann expands \(G_j(u_j)\).
More explicitly,

\[
G_j(u_j(t))=\sum_{\nu\ge0}\frac{(-\epsilon)^\nu}{(\nu+1)!}
D_t^\nu(G_j^{\nu+1})(t).
\]

Multiplication by \(-\epsilon n_j\) and summation over the original branches
gives (3.1).  The proof does not require \(\deg g\le d\).

## 4. Differentiation preserves zero-cycle parameter content

Let

\[
\mathcal V_C=
\left\{\int_Ch:\ h\in\mathbf C[x]\right\}\subset\mathcal K.
\]

**Lemma 4.1 (monodromy injectivity).**  For every \(q\ge1\), the map

\[
D_t^q:\mathcal V_C\longrightarrow\mathcal K
\]

is injective.

**Proof.**  Suppose \(F=\int_Ch\in\mathcal V_C\) and \(D_t^qF=0\).  On the
regular domain, \(F\) is a polynomial in \(t\) of degree less than \(q\), so
it is single-valued.  Monodromy \(\tau\) around infinity acts as a \(d\)-cycle
on the inverse branches of the monic polynomial \(f\).  Therefore

\[
\frac1d\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\tau^kC=0,
\]

because every branch receives the average coefficient
\((\sum_jn_j)/d=0\).  Single-valuedness gives

\[
F=\frac1d\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\int_{\tau^kC}h
=\int_{\frac1d\sum_k\tau^kC}h=0.
\]

Thus the kernel is zero. \(\square\)

**Corollary 4.2.**  If \(F\in R\otimes_{\mathbf C}\mathcal V_C\), then

\[
\operatorname{cont}_R(D_t^qF)=\operatorname{cont}_R(F)
\qquad(q\ge0).                                      \tag{4.1}
\]

To see this, expand \(F=\sum_\ell b_\ell v_\ell\) in a complex basis of its
coefficient span.  Lemma 4.1 says that the \(D_t^qv_\ell\) remain linearly
independent, so the same parameter coefficients \(b_\ell\) form a basis
expansion after differentiation.  In particular, (3.1) gives

\[
\operatorname{cont}_R(M_\mu)=\operatorname{cont}_R(J_\mu)
\qquad(\mu\ge1).                                    \tag{4.2}
\]

This equality is the step that removes the former degree restriction.

## 5. Cayley--Hamilton recurrence

Work in the free rank-\(d\) algebra

\[
\mathcal A=R[t,x]/(f(x)-t)
\]

with basis \(1,x,\ldots,x^{d-1}\).  Let \(T_g\) be multiplication by
\(g_{\boldsymbol a}\).  No bound on \(\deg g\) is needed: reduction modulo
the monic relation \(f(x)-t\) always gives a \(d\)-by-\(d\) matrix over
\(R[t]\).  Write its characteristic polynomial as

\[
\chi_g(Z)=Z^d+c_1(t;\boldsymbol a)Z^{d-1}
+\cdots+c_d(t;\boldsymbol a),\qquad c_i\in R[t].
\]

Cayley--Hamilton gives in \(\mathcal A\)

\[
g^\mu+\sum_{i=1}^dc_ig^{\mu-i}=0\qquad(\mu\ge d).
\]

Apply the \(R[t]\)-linear zero-cycle functional
\([h]\mapsto\int_Ch\).  Since \(J_0=\int_C1=0\),

\[
J_\mu+\sum_{i=1}^dc_iJ_{\mu-i}=0\qquad(\mu\ge d),     \tag{5.1}
\]

where the last term vanishes when \(\mu=d\).

Multiplication by an element of \(R[t]\) cannot introduce a parameter
coefficient outside the ideal generated by the old coefficients: powers of
\(t\) merely replace \(\int_Ch\) by \(\int_Cf^kh\), which remains in
\(\mathcal V_C\), while the coefficients in \(R\) multiply the existing
generators.  Hence

\[
\operatorname{cont}_R(HF)\subseteq\operatorname{cont}_R(F)
\quad\text{for }H\in R[t],\ F\in R\otimes\mathcal V_C. \tag{5.2}
\]

Induction in (5.1) therefore puts the content of every \(J_\mu\) in the ideal
generated by the contents of \(J_1,\ldots,J_{d-1}\).  Equation (4.2) transfers
the statement unchanged to the Melnikov coefficients.  The reverse inclusion
is tautological, proving Theorem C.

## 6. Lower witnesses

Write
\(Z_0(f;\mathbf Z)=\{(n_1,\ldots,n_d)\in\mathbf Z^d:\sum n_j=0\}\) and
\(Z_0(f;\mathbf C)=Z_0(f;\mathbf Z)\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf C\).
For \(f=x^d\), let \(x_j=\omega^jT\).  In the complexified cycle module take
\(n_j=\omega^j\) and \(g=ax\).  Its Fourier support is the
singleton \(\{d-1\}\), so (3.1) vanishes through order \(d-2\) and is nonzero
at order \(d-1\).  Thus the upper bound is sharp for complex-weight cycles.

For a witness in the integral lattice, let \(p\) be the least prime divisor of \(d\), put
\(q=d/p\), and take \(n_j=p-1\) when \(j\equiv0\pmod p\), and \(n_j=-1\)
otherwise.  Its Fourier support is
\(\{q,2q,\ldots,(p-1)q\}\); for \(g=ax\), the first nonzero order is
\(q=d/p\).  This proves an integer-cycle lower bound, not integer sharpness of
\(d-1\).

## 7. Exact checks and assurance boundary

The generic quotient checker uses perturbation degrees strictly above the
phase degree in symbolic cases \(d=2,n=4\) and \(d=3,n=5\).  It verifies the
characteristic recurrence exactly and separately checks the zero-cycle
monodromy average through degree twelve.  The power-phase screen verifies
rectangular cases \((d,n)=(2,4),(3,5),(4,6)\), coefficient-ideal stabilization,
and the lower witnesses.  These are excluded-range falsifiers for the former
proof, not substitutes for Lemma 4.1 or Cayley--Hamilton.

For a nonmonic phase, division of \(f,t\), and \(g\) by the nonzero leading
coefficient of \(f\) changes the coefficient ring only by an automorphism and
multiplies Melnikov coefficients by units.  The stabilization index is
unchanged, so the degree-box consequence includes all nonconstant phases.

The closest located antecedents are the 2013 zero-cycle paper that identifies
the Bautin-index direction, the 2025 paper that states the degree-box problem,
Batenkov--Yomdin's recurrence-based Bautin theorem, and
Briskin--Yomdin's general algebraic-family framework.  No exact statement of
the bound above was located in the bounded search, but this does not establish
historical priority.  The claim remains an unrefereed theorem candidate until
the proof and novelty have independent specialist review.
