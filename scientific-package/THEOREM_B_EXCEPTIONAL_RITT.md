# The exceptional Ritt-collision amplitude theorem

## 1. Statement

Put

\[
Q(x)=x^4+x,\qquad P(x)=Q(x)^3.
\]

Define the free \(\mathbf C[P]\)-submodules

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_0&=\left\langle x,\ x^7+\frac74x^4,\
                    x^{10}-\frac52x^4\right\rangle,\\
M_1&=\left\langle x^2,\ x^5,\
                    x^{11}+\frac{11}{4}x^8\right\rangle,\\
M_2&=\left\langle x^3,\ x^6,\ x^9\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{1}
\]

Every \(G\in\mathbf C[x]\) has a unique decomposition

\[
G=A_0(P)+A_1(P)Q+A_2(P)Q^2+m_0+m_1+m_2,                 \tag{2}
\]

with \(A_i\in\mathbf C[y]\) and \(m_i\in M_i\).  The reconstructed
polynomial-amplitude/root-span theorem then gives

\[
\boxed{
q(P,G'\,dx)=
\mathbf 1_{A_1\ne0}+\mathbf 1_{A_2\ne0}
+3\sum_{i=0}^2\mathbf 1_{m_i\ne0}.
}                                                         \tag{3}
\]

The five non-trace support blocks are

\[
\{4\},\quad \{8\},\quad
S_0=\{1,7,10\},\quad
S_1=\{2,5,11\},\quad
S_2=\{3,6,9\}.                                           \tag{4}
\]

## 2. Direct module decomposition

The twelve generators

\[
1,\ Q,\ Q^2,\quad M_0\text{ generators},\quad
M_1\text{ generators},\quad M_2\text{ generators}
\]

are monic with pairwise distinct leading degrees \(0,1,\ldots,11\).
Because \(P\) is monic of degree \(12\), any such triangular list is a
basis of the free \(\mathbf C[P]\)-module \(\mathbf C[x]\).  This proves
the existence, uniqueness, and directness of (2).  In particular,

\[
M_i\cap\mathbf C[Q]=\{0\}.                               \tag{5}
\]

## 3. The quartic has full symmetric monodromy

Consider the connected degree-four cover

\[
Q(x)=y.
\]

Its finite critical points are the three distinct roots of
\(4x^3+1=0\).  At such a point \(c\),

\[
Q(c)=c^4+c=\frac34c,
\]

so the three critical values are also distinct.  Moreover
\(Q''(c)=12c^2\ne0\); every finite branch cycle is therefore a
transposition.

The monodromy group is generated by these three finite branch cycles,
because the branch cycle at infinity is the inverse of their product.
The cover is connected, hence this transposition-generated group acts
transitively on the four sheets.  Associate an edge of a graph on the four
sheets to each generating transposition.  Transitivity says that this graph
is connected, and the transpositions along the edges of a connected graph
generate the full symmetric group.  Thus

\[
\operatorname{Mon}(Q)=S_4.                              \tag{6}
\]

The reduced permutation representation

\[
\{(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)\in\mathbf C^4:
  z_1+z_2+z_3+z_4=0\}
\]

is the irreducible three-dimensional standard representation of \(S_4\).

## 4. Every non-composite quartic primitive has all three channels

Let \(R\in\mathbf C[x]\), and let \(x_1(y),\ldots,x_4(y)\) be the four
roots of \(Q(x)=y\).  Reduce the evaluation vector

\[
\bigl(R(x_1(y)),\ldots,R(x_4(y))\bigr)
\]

modulo its constant vector.  This reduced vector is zero exactly when
\(R\in\mathbf C[Q]\): the free decomposition
\(\mathbf C[x]=\mathbf C[Q]\langle1,x,x^2,x^3\rangle\)
makes the equivalence immediate.

If \(R\notin\mathbf C[Q]\), the reduced vector is nonzero.  The span of its
analytic continuations is a nonzero \(S_4\)-invariant subspace of the
standard representation, hence is the whole three-dimensional space by
(6).  The reconstructed root-span/support identity therefore gives

\[
q(Q,R'\,dx)=3,
\]

and the inverse series \(R(\eta(t))\), where
\(Q(\eta(t))=t^4\), has all three non-trace residues

\[
1,\ 2,\ 3\pmod4.                                        \tag{7}
\]

## 5. Fusion from degree four to degree twelve

The normalized inverse of \(Q\) at infinity has the form

\[
\eta(t)=t\,\phi(t^{-3}),\qquad
\phi(z)^4+z\phi(z)=1,\qquad \phi(0)=1.                  \tag{8}
\]

Thus every exponent appearing in \(\eta(t)^j\) is congruent to \(j\)
modulo \(3\).  Inspection of (1) shows that all monomial degrees in \(M_i\)
have the single congruence

\[
\deg_x M_0\equiv1,\qquad
\deg_x M_1\equiv2,\qquad
\deg_x M_2\equiv0\pmod3.                                \tag{9}
\]

Multiplication by \(P(\eta(t))=t^{12}\) preserves this congruence.
Consequently every inverse-series exponent of \(m_i\in M_i\) has the
corresponding residue in (9) modulo \(3\).

The remaining issue is to control the quartic trace channel, which becomes a
non-trace channel after composition with the outer cube.  For
\(R\in\mathbf C[x]\), define the fibre trace

\[
\operatorname{tr}_Q R(y)=\sum_{Q(u)=y}R(u).
\]

If \(p_j(y)=\operatorname{tr}_Q(x^j)\), Newton's identities for
\(x^4+x-y\) give

\[
\begin{gathered}
p_1=p_2=p_5=0,\qquad p_3=-3,\quad p_6=3,\quad p_9=-3,\\
p_4=4y,\quad p_7=-7y,\quad p_{10}=10y,\\
p_8=4y^2,\quad p_{11}=-11y^2.
\end{gathered}                                           \tag{10}
\]

Equivalently, these values follow from
\(p_j=yp_{j-4}-p_{j-3}\) for \(j\ge4\).  Hence every displayed generator
of \(M_0\) or \(M_1\) has zero \(Q\)-trace, while the three generators of
\(M_2\) have the constant traces \(-3,3,-3\).  Since

\[
\operatorname{tr}_Q\!\bigl(F(P)R\bigr)
=F(y^3)\operatorname{tr}_Q R,
\]

arbitrary \(\mathbf C[P]\)-coefficients and sums preserve zero trace on
\(M_0,M_1\), while traces on \(M_2\) remain supported in powers of \(y^3\).

This trace calculation is exactly the missing channel filter.  Indeed,

\[
\frac14\operatorname{tr}_Q R(t^4)
=\frac14\sum_{j=0}^3R\bigl(\eta(i^jt)\bigr)
\]

is the part of \(R(\eta(t))\) whose exponent is divisible by four.  It
vanishes identically on \(M_0\) and \(M_1\).  On \(M_2\) it contains only
powers of \(t^{12}\), hence only the trace residue zero modulo twelve.

If \(m_i\ne0\), equation (5) gives \(m_i\notin\mathbf C[Q]\), so Section 4
shows that its inverse series has every nonzero residue modulo \(4\).
Combining that fact, the trace filter, and (9), the Chinese remainder theorem
gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{module} & \text{residue mod }3
               & \text{three residues mod }12\\ \hline
 M_0 & 1 & \{1,7,10\}\\
 M_1 & 2 & \{2,5,11\}\\
 M_2 & 0 & \{3,6,9\}.
\end{array}                                              \tag{11}
\]

This proves both containment and nonvanishing of every channel in the three
fused support blocks.

For the proper-composition summand, \(Q(\eta(t))=t^4\) gives

\[
A_0(t^{12})+A_1(t^{12})t^4+A_2(t^{12})t^8.
\]

Its non-trace support is therefore \(\{4:A_1\ne0\}\cup
\{8:A_2\ne0\}\).  The five blocks in (4) are pairwise disjoint and partition
the eleven nonzero residues modulo \(12\).  Their union has the cardinality
in (3), and the reconstructed amplitude-support theorem proves the result.

## 6. Consequences

1. Every rank \(0,1,\ldots,11\) occurs.
2. Modulo \(\mathbf C[P]\), rank at most three occurs exactly in the
   proper-composition summand \(\mathbf C[Q]/\mathbf C[P]\) or in one
   \(M_i\), with no nontrivial mixture between these alternatives.
3. A nonzero element of any \(M_i\) has rank exactly three; hidden
   single-channel cancellation is impossible at every coefficient degree.
4. The coefficients in (1) select a triangular \(\mathbf C[P]\)-basis
   compatible with the mod-\(3\) grading.  Their otherwise unexplained
   fractions are exactly the coefficients that kill the quartic trace;
   the trace filter and the \(S_4\) standard representation together supply
   the all-degree conclusion.
