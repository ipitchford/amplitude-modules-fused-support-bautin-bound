# Same-programme hostile review of the uniform Bautin theorem

**Review date:** 27 August 2026  
**Target:** `THEOREM_D_BAUTIN_BOUND.md`  
**Disposition:** **PROOF SURVIVES; COEFFICIENT-CONVENTION AND NOVELTY GATES
REMAIN**

This is an internal adversarial review, not independent specialist review.

## 1. Quantifier audit

The theorem quantifies over every monic phase \(f\) of exact degree \(d\ge2\),
every zero-cycle of \(f\), every finite perturbation degree \(n\), the complete
coefficient family \(g_{\boldsymbol a}=\sum_{r=0}^n a_rx^r\), and every
Melnikov order.  Thus it covers the 2025 source's full box
\(\deg f,\deg g\le m\), rather than only the diagonal stratum.

When \(\deg g>\deg f\), additional perturbed roots tend to infinity.  The
source convention assigns them zero weights, while the continued original
branches used in the Lagrange--Bürmann proof retain the cycle weights.

## 2. Definition audit

The proof controls the ordinary ideal in the perturbation-parameter ring
generated by the parameter content of the algebraic functions \(M_\mu(t)\).
The content is basis-independent over \(\mathbf C\), and the proof does not
pass to radicals.

The 2025 source writes
\((M_1)\subset(M_1,M_2)\subset\cdots\) without fully specifying the ambient
coefficient ring.  The manuscript therefore defines its convention before
the theorem.  Specialist confirmation that this is the intended invariant
remains mandatory.

## 3. Algebra audit

The load-bearing steps survive direct inspection.

1. Branchwise Lagrange--Bürmann gives
   \(M_\mu=(-1)^\mu D_t^{\mu-1}J_\mu/\mu!\) without a degree restriction.
2. If \(D_t^q\int_Ch=0\), the integral is single-valued.  Averaging the cycle
   over the \(d\)-cycle at infinity gives zero because its total weight is
   zero.  Hence \(D_t^q\) is injective on zero-cycle polynomial integrals.
3. Injectivity preserves linear independence of a coefficient-span basis, so
   differentiation preserves parameter content exactly.
4. \(R[t,x]/(f-t)\) is free of rank \(d\) for monic \(f\), even when
   \(\deg g>d\); multiplication by \(g\) has a characteristic polynomial in
   \(R[t][Z]\).
5. Cayley--Hamilton yields a length-\(d\) recurrence for \(J_\mu\), and the
   \(J_0\) term at \(\mu=d\) vanishes for a zero-cycle.
6. Multiplication by \(R[t]\) cannot enlarge content because
   \(t^k\int_Ch=\int_Cf^kh\).

Induction gives the first-\(d-1\) bound.  No parameter is divided out, and no
hidden degree bound remains.

## 4. Sharpness audit

The \(d-1\) witness \(n_j=\omega^j\) uses complex cycle coefficients.  It is
valid only after scalar extension and must not be described as an integer
zero-cycle witness.

For integer cycles, the weights

\[
n_j=p-1\quad(j\equiv0\pmod p),\qquad n_j=-1\quad\text{otherwise}
\]

have first Fourier support at \(d/p\), where \(p\) is the least prime divisor
of \(d\).  This is a lower witness, not integer sharpness.

## 5. Prior-art audit

Batenkov--Yomdin prove finite generation consequences for polynomial
recurrences, and Briskin--Yomdin study Bautin ideals of algebraic families.
The residual candidate contribution is the zero-cycle monodromy-injectivity
lemma that transfers the recurrence generators through the
order-dependent differentiation in the Melnikov formula.

Exact and alias searches in the bounded primary corpus found no statement of
\(\mathcal B_\infty=\mathcal B_{d-1}\) for arbitrary polynomial
perturbations.  Search absence does not establish priority.

## 6. Computational audit

`check_general_bautin_bound.py` passes generic cases
\((d,n)=(2,4),(3,5)\), verifying Cayley--Hamilton and zero-cycle orbit
averaging exactly.  `screen_power_bautin_uniform.py` passes
\((2,4),(3,5),(4,6)\) and representative ideal reductions.  These are early
falsifiers for the newly included range, not universal proof.
